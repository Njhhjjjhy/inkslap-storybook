# Voice and tone

Direct. Second person. Quantified. The Inkslap voice promises specific outcomes in specific minutes and days, addresses the reader as `you`, and earns trust by being competent rather than aspirational.

---

## Voice attributes

| Attribute | What it means | What it sounds like |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Direct** | One idea per sentence. Strong verbs. Active voice. | "Upload your logo." Not "Logos can be uploaded by users." |
| **Quantified** | Specific numbers over general claims. | "15 minutes." Not "very fast." |
| **Informal** | Second-person `you` / `你`, never the formal `您`. | "We compress design and shipping into one moment." Not "Esteemed customers may experience..." |
| **Competent** | Authoritative, not aspirational. We have shipped 500,000 items; we say so. | "We've delivered over 500,000 custom items." Not "Imagine if you could..." |
| **Triplet structure** | Headlines, lists, and value props tend to come in threes. | "Selection, design, ordering. One moment." |

## Tone shifts by context

The same voice flexes by context. Marketing copy leans into the metaphors; product UI strips them out; error messages get plain and procedural.

| Context | Voice flex | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Marketing hero | Confident, metaphor-friendly | "A moment to complete it all." |
| Product UI | Plain, action-oriented | "Upload your logo" |
| Error message | Procedural, no shame | "Your sign-in needs to be refreshed. Please log in again." |
| Helper text | Specific, single fact | "Password must be at least 8 characters, letters and numbers." |
| Empty state | Acknowledge + redirect | "Your bag is empty." (no exclamation point) |
| Loading | None | (a spinner only — no "Loading…" string) |
| Success | Confirm + next action | "Order placed. We've sent a confirmation to hello@yourcompany.com." |

## What to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Instead |
|-------|-----|---------|
| Vague time claims | The brand earns trust by being specific | "in 15 minutes", not "quickly" |
| Formal `您` | The brand is informal across the entire site | use `你` always |
| Italics for emphasis | Italics are not used | use weight 500 |
| "Click here" links | Always describe the destination | "View product details" |
| Exclamation points in UI | The brand is competent, not excited | end with a period |
| "We're excited to..." | Aspirational warm-up costs words | start with the action |
| "Powered by AI" | The AI tool is described by what it does | "Generate design previews from a prompt" |
| Marketing-speak in error messages | Errors should be clear, not on-brand | "Wrong password. Try again." not "Oops, let's try that again!" |

## Things the brand says that other brands don't

These are unusual, deliberate choices:

- **"In a moment"** (`一刻`) — a more poetic time unit than "minutes" or "seconds". Used only in marketing copy.
- **"North Star" + "procurement maze"** — explicit guidance metaphor in the About page.
- **Specific minute counts in headers** — most B2B SaaS says "fast"; we say "15 minutes."
- **The closing imperative** — "Hand the work to us. You just wait for the delivery." Direct enough that it lands; informal enough that it doesn't feel transactional.
- **Decisions with weekly KPI updates surfaced to customers** — explicitly stated in `Speak with data` (`用數據說話`). The brand commits to transparency that most B2B platforms do not.

## Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Always `you` | Second person, informal. Never `we offer you...` constructions. |
| Sentence case headlines | One word capitalised, the rest sentence case. |
| Chinese full stop on Chinese headlines | `。` not `.` on `zh-TW` content. |
| Numbers over adjectives | When you can be specific, be specific. |
| One idea per sentence | Multi-clause sentences are a smell. Split them. |
| No italics, ever | The face does not ship italic; the brand does not use it. |
| One sentence at the end | Pages close with a single declarative sentence as a call to action, not a paragraph. |
